Independent check (second session, from the per-episode result files; greedy rollouts keyed by case id and
adapter):

* The numbers reproduce exactly.
* All 400 gate and audit placements have the same init-state hash across nodes, so the comparison is paired.
* Paired bootstrap (5000 resamples) over the 400 placements:

  | comparison | change | 95% CI | episodes flipped up / down |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | n0 → r02c0 | +5.50 points | [-0.8, +11.8] | 94 / 72 |
  | n0 → r01c0 | +1.25 | [-5.0, +7.5] | 85 / 80 |
  | r01c0 → r02c0 | +4.25 | [-1.8, +10.2] | — |

Reading:

* The direction is positive, but the 95% interval includes 0.
* About 40% of episodes change outcome between two nodes even under greedy decoding. So a paired 400-episode
  comparison has a standard error of about 3.2 points. My first estimate assumed far fewer flips and called the
  gain "about 2.5 SE"; that was wrong, and it is about 1.7 SE.
* The audit alone (+4.5) is the cleaner number. The audit never decides anything, while r02c0's gate carries a
  selection effect.
* The seed replicate and the shuffled-feedback control of addendum 10 decide whether this holds.

Implication for every comparison in this study: per-placement outcomes flip often between nearby policies. Any
claim needs either many placements, or pooling over seeds and rounds, as addendum 8's rule does.
