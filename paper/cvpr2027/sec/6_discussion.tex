\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What we claim.} (1) A gated self-improvement loop for VLA policies is safe at this budget: with fresh paired gates, no run degrades on the sealed test, including runs whose recipe collapses without the gate. (2) Its gains on LIBERO-Goal are small, one to two points, and are not distinguishable from one round of plain RL at equal compute; recursion is the largest ingredient and sits at the edge of its bar. (3) A meta level that edits hyperparameters or harness components does not beat a fixed, tuned procedure, on two studies and two platforms, and the reason is mechanistic: every procedure in the space reaches the same plateau within six rounds. (4) In the code domain the verified loop gains several points and recursion is significant at eight rounds. (5) A published VLA-RL number is inflated by about 17 points by training on the test states.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim that recursion or the verifier are useless for VLA policies: the point estimates are positive on every comparison, and the code-domain result shows that recursion separates only after the frozen generator's practice is used up, which six rounds on LIBERO-Goal may not reach; a registered 12-round study is running. We do not claim that an LLM meta level cannot help: the self-repair study (sealed until 2026-11-08) is the test that can show it, and the paper reports its registered result either way.

\paragraph{For practitioners.} Three things in these records are directly usable. A fresh paired gate turns an unsafe self-training recipe into a safe one at the cost of about one extra evaluation per candidate, and it is the only ingredient whose effect is unambiguous here. Replicate-averaged finals and a pooled initial reference are necessary at this scale: a single 500-episode evaluation carries $\pm 1.5$ points of noise, enough to turn a preliminary pass into a registered fail, as happened to our R3. And an LLM meta level is worth its cost only where the procedure can fail; on a tuned recipe its correct diagnoses buy nothing.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The VLA results are on one simulated benchmark family with a 7B policy and a few-demonstration start; longer horizons, other policies and real robots are untested here (real-robot data collection is in progress). The gate is noisy (sd 3.4 points at 200 episodes), which both limits the meta level and makes the loop conservative; a larger gate costs proportionally more simulation. Evaluation noise on the official test is about $\pm 1.5$ points per evaluation, which is why finals are replicate-averaged and why bars below $+2$ would not be readable at this budget. Some runs were continued after hardware or wall-time interruptions, in each case from a completed round with the same plan; the amendments disclose every such event. Finally, preregistration constrains the analysis but not the design: the seed program and the harness space were chosen by us, and a different space might leave more for a meta level to find.
