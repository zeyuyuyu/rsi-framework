\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What we claim.} (1) A gated self-improvement loop for VLA policies is safe at this budget: with fresh paired gates no run degrades on the sealed test, including runs whose recipe collapses without the gate. (2) Its gains on LIBERO-Goal are small, one to two points, and cannot be told apart from one round of plain RL at equal compute, at six and at twelve rounds; recursion is the largest ingredient, just under its bar at six rounds and far over it at twelve, where the loop without recursion degrades. (2b) The gate's protection is conditional: it holds for recursive practice at this budget and fails over twelve rounds for practice from a frozen policy. (3) A meta level that edits hyperparameters or harness components does not beat a fixed, tuned recipe, on two studies and two platforms, and the reason is visible in the records: every recipe in the space reaches the same plateau within six rounds. (3b) When the recipe fails, a meta level allowed to edit it restores the loop to the hand-tuned level, $+2.7$ points over the gate alone; this is below the $+3$ bar we registered, and a numeric search over the same knobs lands within $1.2$ points of the language model. (4) In the code domain the verified loop gains several points and recursion is significant at eight rounds. (5) A published VLA-RL score is inflated by about 17 points by training on the test states.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim that recursion lifts a VLA policy at this budget: at twelve rounds it keeps the loop from drifting down, which is a different and smaller thing than the gain the code domain shows, and the verifier's estimate is positive but under its bar. We do not claim that the language model's diagnosis, rather than the knob space it edits, carries the repair: its margin over blind search is $+1.2$ points $[-0.6, +2.9]$, and the registered self-repair bar ($+3$ over the gate alone) was missed at $+2.7$.

\paragraph{For practitioners.} Three things in these records can be used directly. A fresh paired gate turns an unsafe self-training recipe into a safe one for the price of one extra evaluation per candidate, and it is the only ingredient whose effect is unambiguous here. Averaged finals and a pooled initial reference are necessary at this scale: a single 500-episode evaluation carries about $\pm1.5$ points of noise, enough to turn a preliminary pass into a registered fail, which is what happened to our recursion claim. And a language-model meta level is worth its cost only where the recipe can fail; on a tuned recipe its correct diagnoses buy nothing, and on a broken one it finds the repair in the first round where a numeric search needs several, with the same end result within noise.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The VLA results are on one simulated benchmark family with a 7B policy and a few-demonstration start; longer horizons, other policies and real robots are untested here (real-robot data collection is in progress). The gate is noisy (sd 3.4 points at 200 episodes), which both limits the meta level and makes the loop conservative; a larger gate costs proportionally more simulation. Evaluation noise on the official test is about $\pm1.5$ points per evaluation, which is why finals are averaged and why bars below $+2$ would not be readable at this budget. Some runs were continued after hardware or wall-time interruptions, in each case from a completed round with the same plan; the amendments disclose every such event. Finally, preregistration constrains the analysis but not the design: we chose the seed recipe and the harness space, and a different space might leave more for a meta level to find.
