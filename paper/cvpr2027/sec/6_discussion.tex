
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our findings highlight the complementary roles of
the three RSI components. Program adaptation can
help recover underperforming policies by repairing
ineffective training procedures. Data recursion
improves learning by generating new experience
with the updated policy rather than a frozen
data generator. Policy selection protects against
harmful updates but cannot repair an ineffective
training program on its own. Together, these
components allow task feedback to guide both
policy updates and future training decisions.

Program adaptation also changes how compute is
spent by combining policy learning with training
program search. On LIBERO-Goal, RSI achieves
higher Fresh success at lower total GPU-hours
when RL's pilot tuning cost is included.
However, this does not imply that RSI training
itself is cheaper. Moreover, differences between
Official and Fresh evaluations highlight the
importance of measuring improvements on
independently sampled states.
